Why headcount matters more here

Unlike a drop-in party, a dinner party usually means a fixed number of seats, a specific amount of food, and a start time people actually need to show up for. A link with a live RSVP tally takes the guesswork out of "are we cooking for six or ten" — you can see who's confirmed at a glance instead of cross-referencing texts.

What to include

  • A firm start time — dinner parties run on a schedule more than most casual get-togethers.
  • Dietary notes — ask guests to flag allergies or preferences in advance via your contact info, or just ask in the message.
  • Whether to bring something — a bottle of wine, a side dish — worth stating plainly so nobody shows up empty-handed by mistake, or everyone brings the same thing.
  • Parking or building notes, especially for a first-time guest.
